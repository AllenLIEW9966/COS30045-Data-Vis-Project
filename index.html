Do good reviews sell games?

Each bubble is one game — position by review score, size by units sold. Click a platform in the legend to filter. The answer is messier than you would hope: plenty of 90-rated games sold nothing, and some of the biggest sellers reviewed badly.

What the data shows

  • 1 The Xbox 360 launched a year early and led at first — then the Wii and DS overtook it, and Nintendo never gave the lead back.
  • 2 Handhelds were not a side-show. The DS alone shipped more software than either the PS3 or Xbox 360 in this window.
  • 3 Action and Sports dominate every platform, but each machine has a distinct genre signature visible in the heatmap.
  • 4 Review score and sales correlate weakly — the scatter is a cloud, not a line. Distribution and audience beat quality.
  • 5 On Steam today, free-to-play titles hold the biggest concurrent player counts while paid $10–20 games score highest on reviews.

Where the numbers come from

Historical sales, review scores and playtime come from the CORGIS Video Games dataset (1,212 titles, 2004–2008, compiled from VGChartz, Metacritic and HowLongToBeat).

Live player counts, owner estimates and review ratios come from the SteamSpy API, captured for the top 3,000 titles.

Everything here is real data. Nothing was invented or filled in — see About for the full method.
